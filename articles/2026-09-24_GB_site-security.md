# Construction Site Security in 2026: What Guards, Mobile Patrols and Monitored CCTV Cost – and What Drives the Price

An empty building site at night holds plant, tools, fuel and materials, and it is also a place where trespassers can get hurt. That is why most contractors and developers arrange construction site security for evenings, weekends and holiday shutdowns. The options range from a guard at the gate to cameras watched remotely, and prices vary widely. This guide explains the main options, how each is usually priced and what to check before signing a contract.

## Construction site security: the main options

On-site security services for construction usually come in four forms, and many sites use more than one:

- a static guard who stays on site, controls the gate and makes regular rounds;
- a mobile patrol that visits the site several times a night;
- camera towers or fixed cameras connected to a remote monitoring centre;
- a combination, for example cameras every night and a guard during high-risk periods.

Some providers also offer keyholding and alarm response, meaning someone attends when an alarm is triggered. The right mix depends on the size of the site, what is stored there, how easy it is to reach from the road and how long the project will run.

## Security guard hire: hourly rates and minimum shifts

Security guard hire is normally charged per hour, per guard. As a rough guide, a licensed static guard often costs somewhere between £16 and £25 an hour on a standard weekday. Rates tend to be higher for night shifts, noticeably higher at weekends, and bank holidays are often charged at time and a half or double. A guard with a dog, a supervisor or a guard with extra duties such as first aid or traffic marshalling costs more.

Most providers set a minimum shift, commonly eight to twelve hours, and sometimes a minimum contract length. The site also has to provide basic welfare for the guard: a lit, heated cabin, a toilet and a way to call for help. Where these are missing, they may be added to the price.

## Overnight security and weekend cover

The number of hours is the biggest driver of cost. A few typical patterns:

- nights only: for example 6pm to 7am, around 13 hours a night, or about 65 hours over a working week;
- weekends: from Friday evening to Monday morning, roughly 60 hours;
- 24/7 cover: 168 hours a week, usually split across several guards;
- holiday shutdown: the whole site closed for one or two weeks, often at premium rates.

For illustration, at £18 an hour, nights only across a working week come to well over £1,000 before VAT, and full 24/7 cover to roughly three times that or more. This is why many sites use overnight security from a guard only during the riskiest phases and rely on patrols or cameras the rest of the time.

## Mobile security patrols: visits per night

A mobile security patrol is usually priced per visit rather than per hour. An officer drives to the site, checks fences, gates, cabins and plant, records the visit and moves on to the next site. As a rough guide, a visit often costs somewhere around £15 to £40, depending on how long the officer stays, the distance and the time of night. Two or three visits at irregular times are common.

Patrols cost far less than a static guard, but nobody is on site between visits. They suit smaller sites, sites with secure compounds and projects in quieter phases.

## Site security cameras and remote monitoring

Site security cameras are often supplied as mobile towers with their own power and a mobile data connection, so they work before mains power is connected. When the system detects movement, operators at a monitoring centre see live images, can issue an audio warning through a speaker and, if needed, alert the keyholder or the police.

Towers are usually hired by the week. As a rough guide, a monitored tower often costs from around £80 to £250 a week, plus delivery, installation and collection. The price depends on the number of cameras, the power source, the length of hire and whether monitoring covers every night or only set hours. Before choosing, it is worth asking how quickly alerts are handled and what happens after one.

## CCTV and security combined: when it pays off

Combining CCTV and security staff often gives better cover per pound than either alone. Common set-ups are cameras every night with a patrol that responds to alarms, or cameras plus a guard at the gate during deliveries and while high-value plant or materials are on site. A combination tends to make sense on large or open sites, where thefts have already happened, or during phases when copper cable, tools and fittings are stored before installation.

## Site security services: what a contract usually covers

Site security services are easier to compare when every quote is based on the same brief. Before signing, it helps to check:

- whether a site survey and risk assessment are included;
- written instructions for the guards: gate times, rounds, key control and who to call;
- reporting: patrol logs, incident reports and how quickly they are sent;
- response times for alarms and patrols, stated in the contract;
- how staff are vetted and licensed, and the provider's insurance;
- minimum hire periods, notice periods and extra charges for nights, weekends and bank holidays;
- an itemised price: hourly rates, visits, equipment and monitoring shown separately.

In short, the cost of construction site security depends on hours, risk and the mix of people and technology. A clear brief and itemised quotes make it easier to see which offer fits the site.

This article is for information only. All prices are rough guide ranges for 2026, exclude VAT and are not quotes; actual costs depend on the site, hours, risk level, contract length and provider. Prices quoted in euro follow the same logic.
