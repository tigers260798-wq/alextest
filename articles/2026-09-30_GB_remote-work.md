# Work From Home Jobs for Seniors Over 60 UK: 6 Types of Remote Work and How to Check an Employer Is Genuine

More people in their sixties and seventies are looking for work they can do from a desk at home: no commute, no long shifts on their feet, and hours that fit around family, volunteering or simply a slower pace. Work from home jobs for seniors over 60 UK employers advertise usually fall into a handful of familiar groups. This guide explains what each type of role involves, how employee and freelance arrangements differ, and how to tell a genuine opportunity from a scam.

## Six types of remote work that often suit people over 60

- Data entry and typing. Keying information into spreadsheets, databases or online forms. Accuracy and patience matter more than speed, and most roles need only standard office software.
- Customer service and call handling. Answering phone calls, emails or live chat for a company, usually with a headset and a script or knowledge base to follow. Life experience and a calm manner are real strengths here.
- Content moderation. Reviewing posts, comments, reviews or images against a website's rules. The work is steady but can include upsetting material, so it is worth asking what kind of content a role covers.
- Transcription. Turning recordings of meetings, interviews or dictation into written text. Good hearing, accurate typing and a pair of headphones are the basic kit.
- Proofreading and editing. Checking documents, reports, websites or books for errors. Former teachers, secretaries and anyone with a sharp eye for detail often find this a natural fit.
- Admin support and virtual assistance. Managing diaries, emails, bookings and simple paperwork for small businesses or busy professionals.

## Part time work from home: how the hours usually work

Many of these roles are offered as part time work from home, from a few hours a week up to several days. Some set fixed shifts, particularly call handling, where cover is needed at set times of day. Others, such as transcription or proofreading, are task-based: work arrives in batches and can be done at any hour, as long as the deadline is met.

Before applying, it helps to decide how many hours can realistically be offered each week, and whether fixed shifts or flexible tasks fit daily life better.

## Employee, freelance or platform work

Remote roles come in three main forms, and the difference matters:
- Employee. A contract with one company, pay through the payroll with tax already deducted, paid holiday, and often equipment provided.
- Freelance or self-employed. Work for several clients, an invoice for each job, and tax handled through a self-assessment return. More freedom, but no holiday pay or sick pay.
- Platform work. Online marketplaces that match freelancers with short tasks. Easy to join, but the platform usually takes a fee and competition for tasks can be strong.

## What a genuine role usually asks for

Most home-based roles expect a reliable computer, stable broadband, a quiet space to work and basic confidence with email, video calls and simple software. Call-handling roles often ask for a wired internet connection and a headset. Genuine employers hold an interview, usually by phone or video, ask for proof of the right to work in the UK and send a written contract or terms before any work begins.

## Where to look for online jobs from home

A sensible first step is to find a jobs board that lets you filter by remote or home-based roles and by part-time hours. Established recruitment agencies, the careers pages of well-known companies and specialist boards for older workers are other good places to look.

When browsing online jobs from home, it pays to read the whole advert. A genuine listing names the company, describes the tasks, states the hours and explains how pay is calculated.

## How to check an employer is genuine

Older jobseekers are a frequent target of fake job adverts. A few checks take only minutes:
- Look the company up and confirm it has a registered name, a physical address and a landline, not just a mobile number or a messaging app.
- Make sure there is a named person to speak to and a proper interview.
- Never pay anything upfront. A genuine employer does not charge for training, starter kits, software or background checks.
- Do not share bank card details, PINs or copies of documents before a written offer arrives.
- Be wary of any request to receive parcels and send them on, or to pay a cheque into an account and transfer part of the money back.
- Walk away from pressure to decide on the spot.

Adverts promising that anyone can make money from home with little effort and no experience are the clearest warning sign of all. Real remote work is ordinary work done in a different place, with the same contracts, checks and paperwork as an office job.

## Tax, pension and benefits

Working past State Pension age does not reduce the State Pension itself, and employees over that age no longer pay National Insurance on their wages. Income tax still applies to earnings above the personal allowance, and extra income counts towards means-tested support such as Pension Credit or help with council tax. Anyone receiving benefits should check how new earnings would affect them before accepting a role.

## A quick self-check before applying

Five questions help narrow the choice:
- Phone work or written work?
- Fixed shifts or flexible tasks?
- One employer or several freelance clients?
- Are the computer and broadband good enough for video calls?
- How many hours a week feel comfortable?

Answering them first makes it easier to pick the right adverts, prepare for an interview and spot any offer that does not add up.
