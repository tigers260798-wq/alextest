# Private Cataract Surgery Cost UK 2026: NHS vs Private, and the Glasses Bill Many Forget

Cataract surgery is one of the most common operations in the UK, and most people who have it are over 60. The operation itself is short and routine, but the questions around it are not: how long the wait is, what the private cataract surgery cost UK clinics quote actually includes, and why so many people still need new glasses once both eyes have healed. This guide walks through both routes in plain terms and ends with the part that is often forgotten: the glasses budget.

## What a cataract is, and why surgery is the usual answer

A cataract is a gradual clouding of the eye's natural lens. It usually develops slowly, over months or years, and it is a normal part of ageing rather than a sign that something has gone badly wrong. Typical changes include blurred or misty sight, colours that look faded, glare from headlights at night and more frequent changes of glasses prescription.

Eye drops, vitamins and diet do not reverse a cataract. Once it starts to get in the way of daily life, such as reading, driving or recognising faces across a room, the usual answer is a short operation. The cloudy lens is removed through a tiny cut and replaced with a clear artificial lens. It is typically done under local anaesthetic, takes around 15 to 30 minutes per eye, and most people go home the same day. When both eyes need treatment, they are normally done a few weeks apart.

## Cataract surgery on the NHS: what is included

For people who qualify, cataract surgery on the NHS costs nothing. The decision depends on how much the cataract affects sight and everyday life rather than on a single strict number. The free route normally covers:
- the assessment and eye measurements;
- the operation with a standard monofocal lens;
- eye drops after surgery;
- follow-up checks.

The main drawback is time. Waits from referral to surgery differ a lot between hospitals and often run to several months. The first step is usually a routine sight test with an optometrist, and in England that test is free for everyone aged 60 or over, so the process does not have to start with a bill.

## Private cataract surgery cost UK: what the price covers

The cataract surgery cost UK patients pay privately usually sits somewhere between £2,500 and £5,500 per eye. The spread comes from the clinic, the surgeon, the technology used and, above all, the type of lens chosen. When quotes are compared, it helps to check what the headline price includes:
- the first consultation and scans;
- the operation and the standard lens;
- eye drops and aftercare appointments;
- laser treatment for any later clouding behind the new lens, which some clinics include and others charge for separately.

A low headline price that leaves out aftercare can end up costing more than an all-inclusive package.

## Premium lenses: the extra that changes the glasses bill

A standard monofocal lens is set for one distance, usually far. Premium lenses try to do more:
- toric lenses also correct astigmatism and typically add roughly £300 to £500 per eye;
- multifocal and extended-depth-of-focus lenses aim to reduce the need for glasses at several distances, cost more again and are mostly available privately.

Premium lenses do not suit every eye, and some people notice halos around lights at night with multifocal designs. A careful clinic explains who is and is not a good candidate before any money changes hands.

## Glasses after cataract surgery: reading glasses and varifocals

With a standard lens set for distance, most people see well across a room but still need reading glasses for close work. That is simply how a monofocal lens works, not a sign that the operation failed. The eye needs time to settle, so a new sight test is normally booked around four to six weeks after the second operation, and new glasses are best bought after that point. Between the first and second operation the two eyes can feel unbalanced; some opticians remove one lens from the old glasses as a temporary fix.

People who wore varifocals before surgery often go back to them afterwards, because one pair covers driving, the computer screen and a book. People who receive certain income-related benefits, such as the main part of Pension Credit, can usually get a voucher towards the cost of glasses, so it is worth asking at the sight test.

## Cheap varifocal glasses vs the high street

Varifocals are the most expensive everyday lenses, and high-street prices with branded frames and thinner lenses can run to several hundred pounds. That is why many people now buy varifocals online, where cheap varifocal glasses can cost a fraction of the shop price.

Buying varifocal glasses online works best when a few details are right:
- use a recent prescription, issued after the eyes have settled following surgery;
- have the pupillary distance and, for varifocals, the fitting heights measured, or ask the optician to provide them;
- choose a frame deep enough for the reading zone at the bottom of the lens;
- check the returns and remake policy before paying, because varifocals sometimes need an adjustment period.

Some people keep an online pair as a spare and buy the main pair where it can be fitted and adjusted in person. Either way, comparing the full price, including coatings and thinner lenses, gives a fairer picture than the frame price alone.

## The bottom line

The operation is only part of the cost. The free route saves money but often takes longer; the private route saves time but runs into thousands of pounds per eye, more with premium lenses. In both cases, a realistic plan includes the glasses that follow, from simple readers to a well-fitted pair of varifocals.
