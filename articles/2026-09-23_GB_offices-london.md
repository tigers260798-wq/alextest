# Offices in London: How Much Does an Office Cost and What Drives the Price?

Two teams of the same size can pay very different amounts for an office in London. The postcode matters, but so do the way the space is rented, the number of square feet planned per person and the extra costs that sit on top of the headline rent. This guide explains what drives the price of an office in the capital, with rough ranges for a realistic budget.

## Offices in London: leased, serviced or coworking

Offices in London are rented in three main ways, and each has its own pricing logic.

- A conventional lease: the business rents a floor or part of a floor, usually for five to ten years. Rent is quoted per square foot per year, excluding service charge, business rates and VAT.
- A serviced or managed office: a provider supplies furnished, ready-to-use space with reception, cleaning, internet and meeting rooms. The price is quoted per desk per month and usually covers most running costs.
- Coworking: a membership gives access to a shared space, by the day or by the month.

Comparing these options on the advertised figure alone is misleading. A rent per square foot leaves out several large costs, while a price per desk already includes most of them. The fair comparison is the total monthly cost per person.

## Location: the biggest single factor

London is by far the most expensive market for offices in the United Kingdom, and prices vary sharply within the city. In Mayfair and St James's, the best buildings can command well over £150 per square foot per year. In the City and around King's Cross, good-quality space often costs between £70 and £110. Shoreditch, Farringdon and the South Bank tend to sit a little lower, while Canary Wharf and outer hubs such as Stratford or Croydon can be considerably cheaper, often somewhere between £30 and £55.

Within the same area, the building matters too: a newly refurbished, energy-efficient building rents for more than an older block but may cost less to run, and a short walk to a major station pushes prices up.

## What a lease really costs: rent, service charge and business rates

For most leased offices in the UK, the headline rent is only the first line of the budget. Several costs are added on top:

- service charge: the tenant's share of running the building, including cleaning of common areas, security, lifts, shared heating and cooling and building insurance, often £10 to £20 per square foot per year in central London and more in large towers;
- business rates: a local property tax based on the estimated rental value of the space; in central London the bill can come close to half of the annual rent, while small premises may qualify for relief;
- VAT at 20 per cent on rent and service charge in many office buildings;
- utilities, the tenant's own insurance and cleaning inside the office.

Moving in also has a price: a rent deposit of three to six months is common, and space with only a basic finish needs partitions, kitchens, cabling and furniture, which can cost from tens to well over a hundred pounds per square foot. Landlords often agree to a period without rent at the start of a longer lease. At the end, the space usually has to be returned in its original condition.

## How much office space London teams need per person

The usual rule of thumb is 80 to 120 square feet per person, roughly 7.5 to 11 square metres, including a share of meeting rooms, kitchen and circulation space. Firms with hybrid working and shared desks often plan closer to the lower end, provided there are enough small rooms for calls and focused work.

A worked example: a team of 20 people at 100 square feet each needs about 2,000 square feet. At £75 per square foot, the rent alone is £150,000 a year, or £625 per person per month. Add a service charge of £15 per square foot and business rates at around half the rent, and the running cost rises to roughly £255,000 a year, about £1,060 per person per month, before VAT, utilities and fit-out.

The number of people also shapes safety requirements. Fire exits, alarms, emergency lighting and extinguishers must match the occupancy, and adding more desks than a floor was designed for can mean extra work before moving in.

## Serviced offices London: one price per desk

A serviced office is a private, furnished room or suite inside a building run by an operator, with shared reception, kitchens and meeting rooms. Managed offices go a step further: the operator fits out a whole floor for a single company and runs it on its behalf.

The price is quoted per desk per month. In the City and around King's Cross, it often falls between £500 and £900 per desk; in Mayfair and the wider West End it can reach £800 to £1,500; in outer areas, £300 to £500 is common. The figure looks high next to a rent per square foot, but it usually bundles rent, service charge, business rates, utilities, furniture, cleaning and internet, with no fit-out to fund. Agreements run from a few months to two or three years; meeting rooms and IT upgrades are often extras.

## Coworking and flexible offices London: hot desk, fixed desk or private room

Coworking suits freelancers, small teams and companies testing a new market. The price depends mainly on how much privacy is needed:

- a hot desk with no fixed place often costs £20 to £50 for a day pass or £150 to £400 a month;
- a dedicated desk with storage is commonly £300 to £600 a month;
- a small private room inside a coworking space is priced per desk, often £500 to £900 a month in central areas.

Internet, energy, cleaning and communal areas are usually included; meeting rooms or a registered business address may cost extra. Short commitments are part of what members pay for. For a stable team settling in for several years, a conventional lease usually becomes cheaper once the fit-out has been paid off; for a small or fast-changing team, the all-inclusive options avoid tying up cash in deposits and building work.

## Frequently asked questions

How much does an office for ten people cost in London?
It depends on the format. In a serviced office or a private coworking room, roughly £4,000 to £10,000 a month is common in central areas, all-inclusive. On a conventional lease, the rent alone for around 1,000 square feet can be similar, before service charge, business rates and fit-out.

Is the service charge included in the rent?
Not on a conventional lease: rent, service charge and business rates are billed separately, and VAT is often added.

How long is a typical office lease?
Usually five to ten years, often with a break option part-way through.

Can the price be negotiated?
Yes, particularly on a conventional lease: a period without rent, a contribution to fit-out or a lower deposit are often negotiable, especially when space has been empty for a while.
