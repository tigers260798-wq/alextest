# At-Home Massage Service in 2026: What a Session Includes, What Affects the Price and How to Choose a Therapist

Booking a massage no longer has to mean a trip to a spa. An at-home massage service sends a qualified therapist with a portable table, fresh towels and oils, and the session takes place in a living room or spare room instead of a treatment room. Here is how home visits work, what a session includes, which types of massage are usually offered, what the price depends on and what to check before booking an at-home massage therapist for the first time.

## How an at-home massage service works

There are two main ways to book:

- Booking platforms and apps: the customer chooses the type of massage, the length and a time slot; the platform sends a vetted therapist and handles payment. Choice is wide in large cities, and same-day slots are common.
- Independent mobile therapists: self-employed therapists who cover a set area and take bookings by phone, message or through their own website. Many clients book the same therapist regularly.

Most services ask for the address, parking or access details and a short health questionnaire before the first visit.

## What a session includes

The therapist usually arrives 10 to 15 minutes before the start time to set up. Everything needed comes along: a folding massage table with a face cradle, clean linen, towels, oils or lotions and sometimes a small speaker for quiet music. The home only needs a clear space of about two by three metres, a comfortable room temperature and a socket nearby.

Sessions typically last 60, 90 or 120 minutes. Sixty minutes is the usual minimum, because travel time is part of every visit. After the session, the table is packed away and the room is left as it was.

## Types of massage offered at home

- Swedish or relaxing massage: long, flowing strokes with light to medium pressure; the most booked option for a first session.
- Deep tissue massage: slower, firmer pressure focused on tight areas of the back, neck and shoulders.
- Mobile sports massage: popular with runners, cyclists and gym regulars, booked before an event or after heavy training; the therapist often combines massage with stretching.
- Aromatherapy massage: a relaxing massage with a blend of essential oils chosen at the start of the session.
- Pregnancy massage: done lying on the side with extra cushions, and only by therapists trained in it; many offer it only after the first trimester.
- Couples and group bookings: two therapists at once, or one therapist seeing several people one after another, for birthdays, hen parties or office wellness days.

## What affects the price

An at-home session usually costs more than the same treatment in a salon, because the therapist's travel and setup time are included. The price mainly depends on six things:

- the length of the session: 90 and 120 minutes usually cost less per minute than 60;
- the type of massage: sports and deep tissue work often cost a little more than a relaxing massage;
- the timing: evenings, weekends and bank holidays often carry a surcharge;
- the city: prices in central London are typically the highest in the UK;
- the travel distance: many therapists add a fee outside their usual area;
- the extras: a second therapist, hot stones or a last-minute booking.

Packages of five or ten sessions, and regular bookings with the same therapist, often come with a discount.

## How to choose an at-home massage therapist

A professional massage therapist in the UK usually holds at least a Level 3 diploma in massage, and sports massage therapists often hold a Level 4 or Level 5 qualification. Before the first visit, it is worth checking:

- qualifications for the chosen type of massage;
- insurance: professional and public liability cover is standard for mobile therapists;
- membership of a professional association, which usually requires qualifications, insurance and a code of conduct;
- reviews from other clients, ideally recent ones;
- whether a health questionnaire is sent before the session, a good sign of a careful therapist;
- clear prices, including travel fees and cancellation terms.

Platforms usually check documents in advance. With an independent in-home masseuse or masseur, it is reasonable to ask to see certificates and proof of insurance before the booking is confirmed.

## Safety, comfort and when to check with a doctor first

Professional therapists follow clear rules: the client undresses in private, towels cover everything except the area being worked on, pressure is adjusted on request, and the session can be paused or stopped at any time. For a first visit with a new therapist, sharing the booking details with someone else in the household is a simple extra step.

Massage is not a substitute for medical care. People who have recently had surgery, a blood clot, a fever or a skin infection, a serious heart condition, or who are in the early months of pregnancy are usually asked to get a doctor's advice before booking, and the therapist may adapt or decline the session.

A clear booking, a qualified therapist and an honest health questionnaire make a massage at home just as professional as a visit to a spa, without the journey back afterwards.
