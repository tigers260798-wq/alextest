# The 9-Question Mood Test Doctors Use (PHQ-9): What the Score Means and Which Treatments Exist in 2026

In many doctors' offices, a conversation about mood starts with a short form: nine questions, four possible answers each, about three minutes to fill in. It is called the PHQ-9, and its score runs from 0 to 27. Below are the nine questions, how doctors read each score range, and how the main forms of depression therapy compare in 2026 – from talk therapy and online sessions to TMS, with a look at what they cost.

## The 9 questions doctors use

Each question covers the last two weeks and has four answers: not at all (0 points), several days (1), more than half the days (2), nearly every day (3). The nine topics are:

- Little interest or pleasure in doing things
- Feeling down, depressed or hopeless
- Trouble falling or staying asleep, or sleeping too much
- Feeling tired or having little energy
- Poor appetite or overeating
- Feeling bad about oneself, or feeling like a failure
- Trouble concentrating, for example on reading or television
- Moving or speaking noticeably slowly, or the opposite: being unusually restless
- Thoughts of self-harm or of being better off dead

A tenth question asks how much these problems get in the way of work, home life and relationships. It is not added to the score, but doctors use it to judge the impact on daily life.

## How the 0–27 score is read

The nine answers are added up. Doctors commonly read the total in five ranges:

- 0 to 4: minimal symptoms
- 5 to 9: mild
- 10 to 14: moderate
- 15 to 19: moderately severe
- 20 to 27: severe

A total of 10 or more is the usual threshold for at least moderate symptoms and the point at which doctors typically discuss treatment. The score is a screening tool, not a diagnosis. Thyroid problems, anemia, sleep apnea or grief can push the numbers up too, which is why a doctor looks at the full picture.

The last question is handled separately. Any answer other than "not at all" on thoughts of self-harm is a reason to speak with a doctor the same day, whatever the total. Crisis lines answer around the clock and free of charge: 988 by call or text in the US and Canada, 116 123 in the UK, 13 11 14 in Australia.

## Depression therapy: what talk therapy sessions involve

Talk therapy is the most common starting point, and for mild to moderate symptoms it is often the first option doctors suggest. Therapy sessions for depression usually last 45 to 60 minutes, once a week, over roughly 8 to 20 weeks. The most widely used approaches are:

- Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT): works on thought patterns and daily routines, with small tasks between sessions.
- Interpersonal therapy: focuses on relationships, conflicts and life changes such as a move, a loss or retirement.
- Behavioral activation: gradually brings back activities that were dropped, starting with small steps.

Progress in depression therapy is usually measured along the way: many therapists repeat the same 9-question form every few weeks to see whether the score is moving.

## Online therapy for depression: how it works

Online therapy for depression uses the same methods by video, phone or messaging. It removes travel time and waiting rooms, and evening slots are easier to find. Points worth checking before signing up:

- whether the therapist is licensed in the country or state where the client lives
- whether sessions are live or mostly text-based
- the price per session or per month, and whether insurance reimburses it
- what happens in an emergency, since online services are not crisis services

Some services charge a monthly subscription; others bill per session, like an office visit.

## TMS therapy for depression: when earlier steps are not enough

When at least two earlier treatments have not brought enough improvement, doctors speak of treatment resistant depression. One of the options at that stage is TMS, transcranial magnetic stimulation. A coil placed against the head sends magnetic pulses to an area of the brain involved in mood regulation. The person stays awake, needs no anesthesia and can go home right after the session.

A standard course of TMS therapy for depression runs five days a week for about four to six weeks, with sessions of 20 to 40 minutes; newer protocols shorten each session to a few minutes. The most common side effects are scalp discomfort and headache; seizures are rare. Results vary from person to person, which is why TMS is usually offered after other approaches have been tried.

## Treatment for anxiety and depression: costs with and without insurance

Anxiety and depression often appear together, and most therapists work on both in the same sessions. Treatment for anxiety and depression is paid for differently depending on the country:

- US: most health plans cover mental health care on terms similar to physical care. Without coverage, a therapy session often costs $100 to $200. TMS is usually covered only after other treatments have been tried and the insurer has approved it in advance; a full course without insurance can run to several thousand dollars.
- UK: talking therapies are free through the public health service, and in England people can usually refer themselves without seeing a GP first.
- Canada: visits to family doctors and psychiatrists are publicly covered, while psychologists and counselors are usually paid privately or through workplace benefits.
- Australia: with a mental health plan from a GP, the public system subsidizes up to 10 individual sessions a year with a psychologist or another eligible provider.

The 9-question form is a starting point, not a verdict. Written-down answers make the first appointment faster and more precise, and the same form, repeated later, shows whether the chosen treatment is making a difference.
