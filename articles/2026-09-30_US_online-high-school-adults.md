# Online High School for Adults: 3 Ways to Finish, Which Options Are Free and How to Spot a Fake Diploma

Finishing high school later in life is more common than many people think, and today much of the work can be done from home. Online high school programs for adults follow three main routes: a diploma through a public adult education program, an accredited online high school, or a high school equivalency test such as the GED. All three lead to a credential that most colleges and training programs accept. This guide compares the routes, explains what is usually free and shows how to avoid schools that sell diplomas with no value.

## Three routes at a glance

- Adult diploma through a public program. School districts and adult education centers in many states let adults earn the credits they are missing. An adult high school diploma online through a public program is often free or low-cost for residents.
- Accredited online high school. Private online schools enroll adults year-round, evaluate old transcripts and offer self-paced courses with teacher support. Tuition applies, but the schedule is very flexible.
- High school equivalency test. The GED, and in some states the HiSET, measure high-school-level skills through a set of subject tests. No credits are needed; passing the tests earns a state-issued equivalency credential.

## Online high school diploma for adults: how it works

An online high school diploma for adults usually starts with a transcript review. The school counts the credits already earned, sometimes decades ago, and builds a plan for the rest. Typical requirements include English, math, science, social studies and a few electives.

Courses are self-paced, with an assigned teacher to answer questions and grade work, and tests are often proctored online. How long it takes depends almost entirely on how many credits are missing: some adults finish in a few months, others spread the work over one or two years around work and family. At the end, graduates receive a regular high school diploma rather than an equivalency certificate.

## Online high school for adults free: where no-cost options exist

Online high school for adults free of charge is available more often than many people expect, although eligibility rules apply:

- public adult education programs, funded by states and school districts, which often cost nothing for residents;
- some public libraries, which offer card holders scholarships for an accredited online diploma program;
- free preparation classes for the equivalency test at adult learning centers and community colleges;
- free practice tests and study materials online.

What usually costs money: tuition at private online high schools, and the equivalency test fee, charged per subject at a rate that varies by state. Some states reduce or cover the fee, for example for residents who complete a prep class.

## Online GED programs or a diploma: how they compare

Online GED programs prepare adults for four subject tests: mathematical reasoning, reasoning through language arts, science and social studies. Each test can be taken separately, and in most states the tests can be taken online at home with a webcam after reaching a qualifying score on the official practice test. The usual minimum age is 18, with exceptions for younger candidates in some states. Preparation takes anywhere from a few weeks to several months.

The two routes compare like this:

- What it takes: a diploma means completing the missing courses; the GED means passing four tests.
- Time: the GED is often faster for adults with solid reading and math skills; a diploma suits those who prefer coursework to exams.
- Cost: public diploma programs are often free; the GED has a fee per subject that varies by state.
- Recognition: most colleges accept either, but some programs have their own rules, so it is worth checking in advance.

## Accredited online high schools for adults: how to spot a diploma mill

Accredited online high schools for adults are reviewed by recognized accrediting organizations, and their diplomas are accepted by colleges. Diploma mills imitate them. Six warning signs:

- a diploma promised within days or weeks, for a single payment;
- credit for life experience instead of courses and tests;
- no teachers, no graded assignments and no proctored exams;
- an accreditor that nobody else recognizes, sometimes created by the school itself;
- pressure to pay today to lock in a discount;
- no clear physical address and no transcript policy.

Before enrolling, it helps to confirm that the accreditor is recognized and that the state accepts the diploma, and to ask a community college whether it would accept that diploma for admission.

## First steps

- Request old transcripts from the last high school attended; many schools handle this online.
- Choose the route: a diploma or an equivalency test.
- Check eligibility for free programs: age, residency and enrollment dates.
- Take a practice test to see which subjects need the most work.
- Set a weekly schedule, even if it is only a few hours.

Whichever route is chosen, a finished diploma or equivalency credential opens the door to college courses and further training, and it is never too late to complete it.
