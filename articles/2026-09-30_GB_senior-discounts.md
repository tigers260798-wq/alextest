# Bus Pass Over 60 New Rules in 2026, and 6 Other Discounts Many People Miss

Turning 60 in the UK opens the door to a surprising number of discounts and free services, from travel to prescriptions to broadband. A few arrive automatically, but most have to be asked for, and plenty go unclaimed simply because people do not know they exist or assume they only start at 65 or 66. This guide explains what is changing with the bus pass and sets out six other savings worth checking.

## Bus pass over 60 new rules: what is changing

The free bus pass works differently in each part of the UK:
- In England, free off-peak bus travel starts at State Pension age, not at 60. Off-peak normally means after 9.30am on weekdays and all day at weekends and on bank holidays.
- In Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, free bus travel starts at 60.
- In London, a separate 60+ card, issued for a one-off fee, gives free travel on buses, the Underground and most rail services in the capital from 60, until the main pass takes over at State Pension age.

The change behind the searches for bus pass over 60 new rules is the rise in State Pension age. It is moving from 66 to 67 in stages between 2026 and 2028, depending on date of birth, and because the English bus pass is tied to it, people born from April 1960 onwards will wait a little longer for their pass than earlier generations did. Some councils add their own extras, such as travel before 9.30am or a pass for a companion, so the rules of the council that issues the pass are worth reading when applying.

A free bus pass for over 60s is also worth applying for early: the card takes time to arrive, and the application usually needs a recent photo and proof of age and address.

## 1. The Senior Railcard

Anyone aged 60 or over can buy a Senior Railcard, which currently costs £35 for a year. The senior railcard discount is a third off most standard and first class rail fares across Great Britain, including off-peak and advance tickets. The card pays for itself after a few longer journeys, and it can be kept on a phone or as a plastic card.

## 2. The 3 year Senior Railcard

The 3 year Senior Railcard offers the same discount for a lower average yearly cost than buying three one-year cards. Railcard promotions appear from time to time, so 3 year senior railcard offers are worth watching for, especially before a birthday or a planned trip.

## 3. Coach cards for over 60s

Several coach operators sell their own over-60 cards, typically cutting a third off fares, and some offer cheaper midweek travel as well. For long trips, a coach with a discount card is often the cheapest way to travel.

## 4. Free prescriptions over 60

In England, free prescriptions over 60 apply automatically: the age box on the back of the prescription form is ticked, and no certificate is needed. In Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, prescriptions are free at any age. Anyone in England who pays for a lot of prescriptions before turning 60 can save with a prepayment certificate until then.

## 5. Free sight tests

In England, NHS sight tests are free for everyone aged 60 or over, and in Scotland they are free at any age. A regular test checks not only the prescription but also the health of the eyes, and people who receive certain income-related benefits can also get help towards the cost of glasses.

## 6. Cheap internet for pensioners

Most large broadband providers offer social tariffs: cheaper packages for people who receive certain benefits, including Pension Credit. These usually cost roughly £12 to £25 a month, and switching to one with the same provider normally carries no exit fee. For anyone looking for cheap internet for pensioners, this is often the biggest single saving on the list, yet many eligible households still pay the standard price.

## Pension Credit: the key that unlocks more

Many of the most valuable savings are linked to Pension Credit, a top-up for pensioners on lower incomes. It can bring help with council tax and heating costs, a free TV licence for those aged 75 or over, the broadband social tariffs above and free dental treatment and help with glasses. It is worth checking even with a small private pension or some savings, because the calculation takes more into account than people often expect.

## Everyday extras: shops, leisure and days out

Beyond the big schemes, smaller savings add up:
- cinemas, theatres and many attractions have concession prices for over 60s or over 65s;
- council leisure centres often offer cheaper swimming and gym sessions for older residents;
- some DIY, garden and department stores run weekly discount days for older customers;
- many museums are free anyway, and paid exhibitions often have a concession rate.

The simplest habit is to ask at the till or when booking whether a senior rate exists. A bus pass or railcard is often enough proof of age.

## The bottom line

Discounts for over 60s in the UK are generous but scattered across councils, rail and coach companies, pharmacies and broadband providers. Nobody sends a single letter listing them all, so the savings go to those who ask. A few applications in the year someone turns 60 can add up to hundreds of pounds a year.
