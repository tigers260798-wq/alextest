# Electrician Course for Adults in 2026: Levels, Day or Evening Study, Duration and Cost

Starting an electrician course as an adult usually means fitting study around work and family. Training is organised in levels, runs on daytime, evening, weekend or blended timetables, and varies widely in length and price. This guide explains what each level covers, how long each format tends to take, what the fee is made up of and what to check before enrolling. It describes training only: finishing a course does not by itself lead to a job or to fully qualified status.

## Electrician courses for beginners: starting at Level 1

Level 1 is the entry point and assumes no previous knowledge. Most electrician courses for beginners at this level cover:

- health and safety in a workshop and on site;
- basic principles such as voltage, current and resistance;
- hand tools, cable types and simple fixings;
- wiring basic lighting and socket circuits on practice boards.

Some Level 1 courses are short introductions lasting a few days or weeks; others run as part-time certificates over several months. Adults with practical experience from another trade are often allowed to start directly at Level 2 after an initial assessment. Level 1 alone is not enough to carry out electrical work unsupervised; its main value is showing whether the work suits the person before a larger commitment.

## Level 2 electrician course: the core installation skills

A level 2 electrician course is where most adults begin in earnest. It is usually a diploma in electrical installations and combines theory with a large share of practical work:

- electrical science and the principles of installation;
- installing wiring systems and containment such as conduit and trunking;
- terminating cables and connecting accessories;
- wiring lighting and power circuits and simple distribution boards;
- an introduction to the current wiring regulations.

Assessment combines written or online exams with practical tasks in a workshop bay. Full-time study at a college usually takes around one academic year, an intensive timetable at a private training centre can compress the classroom part into roughly four to eight weeks, and evening study often takes a year or longer. Level 2 is a foundation, not the end of the route: it does not make someone a qualified electrician.

## Level 3 electrician course: design, testing and fault finding

A level 3 electrician course builds on Level 2 and usually requires it, or equivalent experience, for entry. The content becomes more technical:

- circuit design, cable sizing and electrical calculations;
- inspection, testing and commissioning of installations;
- fault diagnosis and repair;
- the wiring regulations in more depth and planning work on site.

The Level 3 classroom diploma proves knowledge and workshop skill. To be recognised as fully competent, learners usually also need a work-based qualification built from evidence gathered on real sites, followed by a final practical assessment. Classroom study at this level takes roughly as long as Level 2: about an academic year full-time, one to two years in the evenings, or several weeks to a few months on an intensive timetable.

## Electrical installation course or a short specialist course?

The phrase electrical installation course normally describes the Level 2 and Level 3 pathway as a whole: how circuits in homes, commercial premises and industrial buildings are planned, installed, tested and handed over. It is a long programme that builds skills step by step.

Short specialist courses last from one to five days and cover a single topic, such as an update on the wiring regulations, inspection and testing, or installing electric vehicle charge points and solar panels. They are designed mainly for people who already hold core qualifications and cannot replace a full electrical installation course. Some providers also offer a shorter route limited to work in homes; its scope is narrower, so it is worth reading exactly what it covers.

## Evening electrical courses, daytime or online: which format fits

The same qualification can usually be studied in several formats. The level stays the same; the timetable and total length change.

- Daytime: weekday study at a college or training centre. This is the fastest classroom route, but hard to combine with a full-time job.
- Evening: evening electrical courses usually run one or two evenings a week, around three hours per session, in a workshop. Level 2 or Level 3 in this format commonly takes around a year, sometimes longer.
- Weekend: Saturday or Sunday blocks, sometimes on alternate weekends, with a total length similar to evening study.
- Online or blended: an online electrician course can cover theory, calculations, regulations and exam preparation. Practical skills still need workshop time, so blended courses add block days or weeks in a workshop.

When comparing formats, the total number of workshop hours says more than the number of weeks. A fully online installation course deserves a careful look, because the practical assessment still takes place in person.

## Cost of an electrician course and what to check before enrolling

The advertised fee is rarely the full cost. The total usually includes:

- tuition for the classroom and workshop part;
- registration, exam and certification fees, sometimes charged separately;
- resit fees if an exam or practical task has to be repeated;
- basic hand tools, safety boots and a copy of the current regulations;
- travel and, for block weeks, time away from paid work;
- the later on-site stage and final practical assessment, often priced separately.

As a broad guide, a short introductory course may cost a few hundred pounds, a single diploma at a private centre often costs from around one thousand to a few thousand pounds, and combined packages run to several thousand. Evening courses at colleges can be cheaper, and depending on age, previous qualifications and income, some adults may get help with fees or a loan for adult learners. The same logic applies to prices in euros.

Before paying, it helps to check:

- the exact title and level of the qualification, whether it is nationally recognised and whether the provider is approved to assess it;
- how many hours are spent in the workshop and whether each learner has a practice bay;
- the equipment: distribution boards, conduit, test instruments and fault-finding rigs in good condition;
- group size and whether the trainers have worked as electricians;
- what the price includes, from exams and resits to materials.

Claims of becoming fully qualified in a few weeks without any site experience deserve particular caution.

## After the course: possible next steps

After Level 1, most learners move on to Level 2, and after Level 2 to Level 3. Some combine study with work as an electrician's mate to gain practical experience, although finding such work is not part of most courses. After the Level 3 diploma, the usual route is to collect evidence of real installation work under the supervision of a qualified electrician, complete the work-based qualification and take the final practical assessment. How long this takes depends on the site work available.

In Ireland, the main route to working as a qualified electrician is a craft apprenticeship with an employer, combining work on site with blocks of off-the-job training over about four years. Courses there are usually introductory or preparatory, and levels follow a different national framework, so a course listed as level 5 or 6 is not the same as a UK Level 2 or 3.

Pay, employment and the time needed to become fully qualified depend on the person and the employer, and no course can promise them. A good course offers a clear level, enough practical time and an honest picture of what comes next.

  - Колледж очно: около одного учебного года на уровень.
  - Частные центры: Level 2 интенсивом около 4–6 недель.
  - Вечером: 1–2 вечера по ~3 ч, уровень примерно за год и дольше.
  - Короткие курсы: 18th Edition ~3 дня; Inspection & Testing (2391) ~5 дней; EV-зарядки 2–3 дня; PAT 1–2 дня.
- **Цены (ориентиры по памяти).**
  - Частный центр: Level 2 ~£2 000–3 500, пакеты Level 2+3 ~£4 000–8 000+.
  - Короткие курсы ~£300–600.
  - Колледжи дешевле.
- **Финансирование.** В Англии — финансирование навыков для взрослых и Advanced Learner Loan на Level 3+. Условия зависят от возраста, прежних квалификаций и дохода.
