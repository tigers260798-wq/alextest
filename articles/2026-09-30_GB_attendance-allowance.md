# Attendance Allowance 2026/27: Up to £114.60 a Week, and Savings Don't Count

Attendance Allowance is one of the few UK benefits for older people that ignores income and savings completely. It is paid to people over State Pension age who need help with personal care or supervision because of an illness or disability. Yet many people who could qualify never claim, assuming a private pension, a paid-off house or savings rule them out. This guide explains the Attendance Allowance rates for 2026/27, who the benefit is for, what else it can unlock and how a claim works.

## What Attendance Allowance is, and what it is not

Attendance Allowance is a weekly, tax-free payment towards the extra costs of needing care. It is not a wage for a carer, and nobody has to have a carer in place to receive it. The money can pay for a cleaner, taxis, extra heating or a personal alarm, and nobody asks how it is spent.

Three points are often misunderstood:
- It is not means-tested, so pensions, savings and property do not reduce it.
- It is ignored as income by most other benefits.
- It does not depend on National Insurance contributions.

## Attendance Allowance rates for 2026/27

From April 2026 the benefit is paid at one of two weekly rates:
- Lower rate: £76.70 a week, when help or supervision is needed during the day or during the night.
- Higher rate: £114.60 a week, when help is needed both day and night, or for someone nearing the end of life.

Over a year the higher rate comes to almost £6,000 and the lower rate to almost £4,000. Payments usually arrive every four weeks, and the rates are reviewed each April.

## Who it is usually for: care needs, not diagnoses

A claim is judged on the help a person needs, not on the name of a condition. Arthritis, sight loss, the effects of a stroke, Parkinson's or dementia can all lead to a successful claim when they create care needs. The questions are practical: is help needed with washing, dressing, getting in and out of the bath, taking medication or moving around the home, and does someone need to keep an eye on the person to stay safe?

Two conditions apply to most claims:
- the person has reached State Pension age;
- the needs have existed for at least six months.

For someone nearing the end of life the six-month rule does not apply, and claims are handled quickly under special rules, always at the higher rate.

The help does not have to be received already. A person who manages alone but takes an hour to get dressed, or avoids having a bath because it feels unsafe, can still qualify.

## What other benefits can I claim with Attendance Allowance?

An award can raise or unlock other support, which often makes it worth far more than its weekly rate:
- Pension Credit. For someone who lives alone, receives Attendance Allowance and has nobody paid a carer's benefit for looking after them, an extra amount for severe disability is added to the Pension Credit calculation. Many people who were just over the income limit become eligible once it is included, so it makes sense to check pension credit eligibility again as soon as an award letter arrives.
- Council Tax Reduction and Housing Benefit, which usually rise in line with Pension Credit.
- Carer's Allowance for a family member who provides at least 35 hours of care a week.
- A council tax discount in some cases of dementia, where the person is treated as severely mentally impaired.

Because pension credit also opens the door to help with heating costs, a free TV licence for those aged 75 or over and other extras, the combined value can reach several thousand pounds a year.

## When care costs grow: care homes and bridging loans for over 70s

Attendance Allowance usually stops after four weeks in a care home when the council helps with the fees, but it can continue for people who pay their own fees.

Families looking for help with care home fees often face the same problem: the money is tied up in a house that has not yet been sold. Some look at bridging loans for over 70s to cover the months between moving in and completing the sale. These loans are secured on property, run until the sale goes through and can become expensive once arrangement fees and monthly interest are added. It is sensible to compare the total cost with other routes, such as an agreement with the council to defer care fees until the home is sold, and to take independent advice before signing anything.

## How to apply for Attendance Allowance online or by post

Many people search for a way to apply for attendance allowance online. A claim can be started online or made on the paper form, which can be ordered by phone or printed and posted back. A relative or friend can fill it in if the person who needs care agrees.

A few habits make a claim stronger:
- Describe a typical bad day, not a good one, and say how long tasks take.
- Mention falls, near misses and any help needed at night.
- Keep a simple diary for a week before filling in the form.
- Keep a copy of everything that is sent.

Payment starts from the date the claim begins; for a paper form this is usually the day it was requested, if it comes back within the time limit. Earlier weeks are not paid, so there is no reason to delay. Decisions often take a couple of months, and a refusal can usually be challenged within one month of the decision letter.

A clear, honest picture of daily needs, sent early, is often the difference between a refused claim and up to almost £6,000 a year of extra support.
