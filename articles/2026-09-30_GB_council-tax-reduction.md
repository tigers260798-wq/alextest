# Council Tax Reduction for Pensioners in 2026: Who Pays Less, Who Pays Nothing and How to Apply

Council Tax is one of the biggest fixed bills in retirement, and it keeps rising while most pensions rise more slowly. Yet a large number of older households pay more than they need to. Council Tax Reduction can cut the bill for pensioners on a modest income and, in some cases, take it down to zero. On top of that, several discounts have nothing to do with income at all. This guide explains how council tax reduction works for people over State Pension age in 2026, which discounts are worth checking and how to apply.

## Council Tax Benefit, Council Tax Reduction, Council Tax Support

The names cause confusion. Council tax benefit was the national scheme until 2013. It was replaced by Council Tax Reduction, which many councils call Council Tax Support. The idea is the same: the council lowers the bill instead of paying out money. Council Tax applies in England, Scotland and Wales; Northern Ireland has a separate system of rates with its own relief.

For people of working age each council in England writes its own scheme, and the rules differ widely. Pensioners are treated differently. Their rules are set nationally, are broadly the same whichever council sends the bill and are more generous than most working-age schemes.

## Who can have the whole bill reduced

The most important link is with Pension Credit. A pensioner who receives the main part of Pension Credit, the part that tops up weekly income to a minimum level, is normally entitled to the maximum reduction. In practice that usually means a council tax bill of £0, whatever the band of the home.

This is one reason why a Pension Credit claim is worth making even when the weekly top-up looks small. In 2026/27 the minimum income level used by Pension Credit is £238.00 a week for a single pensioner and £363.25 for a couple. Someone slightly above those amounts may still qualify once housing costs, disability or caring responsibilities are taken into account.

## Council tax reduction on a modest income

Pensioners who do not get Pension Credit can still receive help with council tax. The council compares weekly income with a set amount for the household and reduces the bill in steps as income rises above it.

Savings matter too:
- the first £10,000 of savings is ignored;
- above that, a small weekly income is assumed for every £500 of savings;
- with more than £16,000 in savings there is normally no Council Tax Reduction, unless the person receives the main part of Pension Credit.

Disability benefits can help rather than hurt. Council tax reduction and Attendance Allowance often go together: Attendance Allowance is not counted as income, and for someone who lives alone it can add an extra amount to the calculation, which increases the reduction.

## Discounts that do not depend on income

Some savings are available whatever the level of income or savings:
- Council tax single person discount. When only one adult lives in the home, the bill is cut by 25%.
- Disregarded adults. Some people are not counted when the adults in a home are added up, including live-in carers and people treated as severely mentally impaired, for example because of advanced dementia. A couple where one partner is disregarded can therefore receive the single person discount.
- Council tax disabled band reduction. If a home has been adapted for a disabled person, for example with an extra bathroom, a downstairs room used by that person or space to use a wheelchair indoors, the bill can be charged as if the home were one band lower. Homes already in the lowest band still receive a reduction.

These discounts can be combined with Council Tax Reduction.

## How to apply for council tax discount

Applications go to the council that sends the bill, not to a central office. Most councils offer an online form, and phone or paper applications are available for anyone who prefers them. Many people look up how to apply for council tax discount and find that one form covers several discounts. It helps to have ready:
- details of all pensions and other income;
- bank and savings statements;
- any benefit award letters, including Pension Credit or Attendance Allowance;
- the names of everyone who lives in the home.

For pensioners, a claim can usually be backdated for up to three months, so there is no advantage in waiting. Once awarded, the reduction appears on the next bill rather than being paid out. Any change in income, savings or who lives in the home should be reported quickly. One more useful step: the annual bill can be spread over twelve monthly payments instead of ten on request, which lowers each instalment.

## Could a smaller home cut the bill?

Help with council tax is not only about discounts. The bill depends on the band of the property, so moving from a large family house to a flat or bungalow in a lower band can reduce it for good, along with heating and maintenance costs. Timing is the hard part: the new home is often found before the old one sells. Some older homeowners use bridging loans for over 70s to buy first and repay when the sale completes. These loans are secured on property and can be costly if a sale drags on, so the full cost should be compared with waiting for the sale, and independent advice taken first.

The simplest step costs nothing: check the band, check who counts as living in the home and make sure every discount is claimed. For many pensioners, those three checks are worth hundreds of pounds a year.
