# The 6-Question ADHD Screener Doctors Use for Adults (ASRS): What the Result Means and How Adults Get Diagnosed in 2026

ADHD is often thought of as a childhood condition, yet many people first look into it as adults, when work, family and deadlines pile up. Before a full adult ADHD diagnosis, doctors often start with a short screener: six questions that take about two minutes, from the Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale, or ASRS. Below: the six questions, how the answers are counted, what the result can and cannot show, and how an ADHD evaluation for adults works in 2026 – who carries it out, how long the wait is and what it typically costs.

## The 6 screening questions and the answer scale

Each question looks back over the past six months and has five possible answers: never, rarely, sometimes, often, very often. The six questions ask how often:

- 1. there is trouble wrapping up the final details of a project once the challenging parts are done;
- 2. it is hard to get things in order when a task requires organization;
- 3. appointments or obligations are forgotten;
- 4. getting started is avoided or delayed when a task requires a lot of thought;
- 5. there is fidgeting or squirming with hands or feet when sitting for a long time;
- 6. there is a feeling of being overly active and compelled to do things, as if driven by a motor.

The first four questions cover attention and organization. The last two cover restlessness, which in adults is often more an inner tension than visible hyperactivity.

## How the result is read – and what a screener cannot do

Each question has its own threshold:

- Questions 1 to 3 count when the answer is sometimes, often or very often.
- Questions 4 to 6 count when the answer is often or very often.

Four or more answers above the threshold mean the pattern is consistent with ADHD in adults, and a full assessment is worth considering. Fewer than four make ADHD less likely, but do not rule it out.

A screener is not a diagnosis. Stress, poor sleep, anxiety, low mood, thyroid problems and other health issues can produce the same answers. The questionnaire also says nothing about childhood, which is central to any diagnosis, and nothing about how much the difficulties affect daily life.

## ADHD evaluation for adults: what a full assessment includes

Before an ADHD assessment, adults are usually asked to fill in longer questionnaires and to bring anything that shows what they were like as children. A full ADHD evaluation for adults then takes one to three appointments and two to four hours in total. It typically includes:

- a detailed interview about current difficulties at work, at home and in relationships;
- questions about childhood, because signs have to have been present before the age of 12, often supported by old school reports;
- longer rating scales, sometimes also filled in by a partner, parent or close friend;
- checks for other explanations, such as anxiety, depression, sleep disorders or learning difficulties, which can also exist alongside ADHD;
- a written report with the result and recommended next steps.

For an adult ADHD diagnosis, several symptoms must be present in two or more areas of life, for example at work and at home, and must clearly affect daily functioning.

## Who carries out an adult ADHD diagnosis

Depending on the country, the diagnosis is made by a psychiatrist, a clinical psychologist or, in some services, a specialist nurse working with a psychiatrist.

- Psychiatrist: can diagnose ADHD and plan follow-up care within the same service.
- Psychologist ADHD assessment: often the most detailed testing, including attention and memory tasks; the report is then shared with the family doctor or a psychiatrist for follow-up.
- Online services: many clinics now run the interviews by video. Quality varies, so it is worth checking who signs the report, whether childhood history is covered and whether the family doctor or GP accepts the report for shared care.

## Waiting times and costs in 2026

Numbers vary widely, but the pattern is similar across English-speaking countries: public routes are cheaper and slower, private routes are faster and more expensive.

- United States: insurance often covers part of an evaluation with an in-network psychiatrist or psychologist. Without coverage, a psychiatric evaluation typically costs a few hundred dollars, while full psychological testing often runs from about $1,000 to $3,000. Waits range from a few weeks to several months.
- United Kingdom: an NHS assessment starts with a GP referral and is free at the point of use, but waiting lists in many areas run to months or years. A private ADHD assessment usually takes weeks rather than years. The typical ADHD private diagnosis cost is roughly £500 to £1,500, with follow-up appointments charged separately.
- Canada: a psychiatrist's assessment is covered by provincial health plans with a doctor's referral, often after a long wait. A private psychologist assessment commonly costs around CA$1,500 to CA$3,000 and is sometimes partly covered by workplace insurance.
- Australia: a psychiatrist's assessment with a GP referral attracts a Medicare rebate, but out-of-pocket costs often reach several hundred dollars and waits can run for months. A psychologist assessment is usually paid privately.

The six questions take about two minutes. A written copy of the answers, together with notes on childhood and on how daily life is affected, makes the first conversation with a GP, family doctor or specialist more productive.
