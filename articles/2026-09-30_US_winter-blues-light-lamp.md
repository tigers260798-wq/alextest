# Winter Blues or Seasonal Depression? A 7-Question Check and What Actually Helps: Light Lamps, Morning Light, a Doctor

When it is still dark at 7 a.m., sleep, energy and mood change for many people. Most of the time this is the ordinary winter blues. For some, it is seasonal depression, known in medicine as seasonal affective disorder, or SAD. Doctors tell the two apart with a handful of questions. Below are seven of them, the reason light therapy lamps are rated at 10,000 lux, what to check before buying one, and the seasonal depression treatment options for when a lamp is not enough.

## Winter blues vs seasonal depression: where the line is

Both follow the calendar: they usually start in October or November and lift in spring. The difference lies in how strong and how steady the symptoms are.

- How often: winter blues comes and goes, with good days in between. Seasonal depression shows up most days, for weeks at a time.
- Daily life: with winter blues, work, family and friendships stay mostly unchanged. Seasonal depression often disrupts them – tasks pile up, plans get canceled.
- Pattern: seasonal depression returns at the same time of year, and doctors look for at least two winters in a row.

Typical winter signs are oversleeping, trouble getting up, cravings for bread, pasta and sweets, and weight gain. That is the opposite of the poor sleep and poor appetite that often come with other forms of depression.

## The 7 questions doctors ask

There is no single scored form for this. These seven questions come up in most conversations about seasonal mood:

- 1. In which months do sleep and energy change the most: October–November, December–January, February–March, or no change at all?
- 2. Has the same pattern appeared in at least the last two winters?
- 3. Does it lift on its own in spring or summer?
- 4. Are the low days occasional, or present most days for two weeks or longer?
- 5. How much more sleep is needed than in summer, and how much harder is getting up?
- 6. Has appetite changed – especially cravings for carbohydrates – or has weight gone up?
- 7. Are work, family life or social plans affected, or is daily life mostly unchanged?

Answers that point to "most days", "at least two winters" and "daily life affected" are the combination doctors take seriously. The check helps prepare for an appointment; it is not a diagnosis. An underactive thyroid or low vitamin D can cause similar tiredness, and a blood test can rule them out.

## Why light therapy lamps are rated at 10,000 lux

Light therapy is one of the standard approaches for the winter pattern. The idea is simple: bright light in the morning helps reset the body clock that short days push off track. An ordinary room lamp gives a few hundred lux. A light therapy lamp for depression delivers 10,000 lux at a stated distance, closer to the brightness of daylight outdoors. Doctors usually describe SAD light therapy like this:

- 10,000 lux of white light, with UV filtered out
- 20 to 30 minutes a day
- within the first hour after waking
- at the distance the manufacturer states, usually 30 to 60 cm (about 12 to 24 inches), eyes open but not staring into the lamp
- every day through the dark months, not only on bad days

Doctors usually suggest judging the effect only after two to three weeks of daily use. Headaches, eye strain or feeling jittery are the usual side effects; a shorter session or a little more distance often solves them. People with bipolar disorder, eye diseases, or medicines that make skin or eyes sensitive to light should speak with a doctor before starting.

## What to check before buying a winter depression lamp

Prices run from under $50 to well over $200, and the most expensive model is not automatically the best light for winter depression. What matters:

- Lux at a stated distance: 10,000 lux at 30 cm is far more practical than 10,000 lux at 10 cm, which forces sitting very close.
- UV filter: the box should say that ultraviolet light is filtered or not emitted.
- Size of the light surface: a larger panel makes the exact position less critical and leaves room to read or eat breakfast at the same time.
- White light: most medical advice refers to bright white light rather than colored light.
- Flicker-free light and adjustable brightness, handy for starting with shorter sessions.
- Timer and stand: a built-in timer and an adjustable angle make a daily routine easier.

A winter depression lamp is not a desk lamp with a strong bulb. Ordinary lamps and daylight bulbs rarely reach 10,000 lux at a usable distance.

## When a lamp is not enough: seasonal depression treatment

Light therapy is one option, not the only one. Treatments for depression with a seasonal pattern include:

- Talk therapy, especially a version of cognitive behavioral therapy adapted to winter, which works on routines, activity and thought patterns during the dark months.
- Morning daylight: a walk outside soon after sunrise, even on cloudy days, adds light that indoor rooms do not provide.
- Regular exercise and fixed sleep and wake times, which support the same body clock the lamp works on.
- A dawn simulator, which slowly brightens the bedroom before the alarm goes off.
- Medical treatment prescribed by a doctor, sometimes started in early autumn before the usual low period returns.

Vitamin D comes up often. A blood test shows whether levels are low, but as a stand-alone seasonal depression treatment the evidence is mixed.

When low mood lasts most days for more than two weeks, or daily life starts to suffer, a family doctor is the right first stop. In a crisis, help is available around the clock: 988 by call or text in the US and Canada, 116 123 in the UK.
