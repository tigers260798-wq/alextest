# Tax Help for Seniors in 2026: Extra Deductions After 65, Property Tax Relief and Free Volunteer Filing Help

Turning 65 changes a household's taxes more than many people expect. There are extra federal deductions, special rules for Social Security income and a patchwork of local programs that can lower the property tax bill on a home. Very little of it happens automatically: most breaks have to be claimed on the return, and many local programs need an application before a deadline. This guide explains the main federal rules, the four common types of property tax relief and where to find reliable tax help for seniors.

## Federal tax breaks after 65

At the federal level, three rules matter most for older filers:
- A larger standard deduction. Filers aged 65 and older add an extra amount to the regular standard deduction, and the extra amount is higher for a single filer than for each spouse on a joint return.
- A temporary senior deduction. For tax years 2025 through 2028, people aged 65 and older can also claim an additional deduction of up to $6,000 each. It shrinks gradually once income passes $75,000 for a single filer or $150,000 for a married couple, and it can be claimed whether or not the filer itemizes.
- Social Security income. Whether benefits are taxed depends on combined income: adjusted gross income plus tax-exempt interest plus half of the benefits. Below $25,000 for a single filer or $32,000 for a married couple filing jointly, benefits are generally not taxed; above those levels, up to 85% of the benefits can become taxable.

Deductions reduce taxable income; they are not payments, and what they are worth depends on each household's tax bracket. Retirement also brings other changes: required withdrawals from many retirement accounts at a certain age, estimated tax payments when no employer withholds tax, and the option to have tax withheld from Social Security benefits.

## Senior property tax relief: four common types

Property tax is set locally, so senior property tax relief looks different from one state, county or city to the next. Most property tax relief programs for seniors fall into four types:
- Homestead exemptions reduce the taxable value of a primary home, often with a larger exemption for older owners.
- Assessment freezes lock the taxable value of the home once the owner reaches a certain age, so a rising market value does not push the bill up.
- Circuit breaker credits limit property tax to a share of household income and give a credit for the amount above that share.
- Deferral programs let eligible owners postpone some or all property tax until the home is sold or the estate is settled, usually with a lien on the home and sometimes with interest.

Eligibility usually depends on age, often 65 or older, on income limits, on how long the owner has lived in the home and on whether it is the primary residence. Two neighboring counties can run very different programs.

## How to apply for property tax assistance for seniors

Property tax assistance for seniors almost always requires an application, and the deadlines are strict. The local office that assesses property usually handles exemptions and freezes, while circuit breaker credits are often claimed together with the state income tax return. Typical documents include proof of age, proof of income for the previous year and proof that the home is the primary residence. Many programs need renewal every year or every few years, and a missed renewal can mean losing the benefit for that year.

## Tax counseling for the elderly and other free help

Paid preparers are not the only option. Tax Counseling for the Elderly is a long-running federal volunteer program in which trained and certified volunteers prepare returns for people aged 60 and older, with a focus on pensions, retirement income and Social Security. A related volunteer program run by a large nonprofit for older adults works in libraries, senior centers and community rooms. Together they are the main source of free tax preparation for seniors, usually from late January to mid-April and often by appointment. Volunteer sites handle common returns; more complex situations, such as rental property or sizable business income, may still call for a paid professional.

Before an appointment it helps to bring:
- photo ID and Social Security numbers for everyone on the return;
- all income statements, including the yearly Social Security benefit statement and pension or retirement account forms;
- last year's tax return;
- property tax bills and records of medical expenses, in case itemizing makes sense.

## Where scams hide

Tax season also brings imposters. Genuine tax offices do not demand immediate payment by gift card, wire transfer or cryptocurrency, and they do not threaten arrest over the phone. Any call, text or email that claims to come from a tax office is best checked independently before any personal details are shared.

## A yearly check

Rules, income limits and deadlines change, and so does a household's income. A short review each year, ideally before the local application deadline for property tax programs and again before tax season, is the most reliable way to make sure available relief is not overlooked.
