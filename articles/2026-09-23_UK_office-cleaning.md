# Office Cleaning in England: Shifts, Duties, Pay Rules and What to Check in a Contract

Office cleaning is part of what the industry calls contract cleaning: the regular cleaning of buildings that are in daily use. Offices, medical practices and shared workspaces are usually cleaned every day or several times a week, mostly when nobody is working there — early in the morning, in the evening or during quiet hours. This guide explains how office cleaning work is organised in England: who employs cleaners, which duties and shift patterns are common, which pay rules apply in 2026 and what to check before signing a contract.

## Who employs office cleaners: the cleaning company

Most businesses do not hire their own cleaners. They sign a contract with a provider, which may be a two-person local firm or a large cleaning company, UK-wide or regional; the same employment law applies to both. The contract includes a cleaning specification that lists which rooms are cleaned, how often and to what standard. The provider then schedules its staff across the sites it serves.

For cleaners, this means the employer is the cleaning company, not the business whose office is being cleaned. On site there is usually a supervisor or site manager who gives an induction — the layout of the building, the cleaning schedule, the products and the safety rules — and later checks the quality of the work. Some hospitals and large organisations employ cleaners directly; their own pay scales and policies then apply.

## What office cleaning services include

Clients buy office cleaning services against a written specification, so the exact duties vary from site to site. Typical tasks include:

- dusting desks and accessible surfaces without moving or reading papers
- vacuuming carpets and mopping hard floors
- emptying bins and separating recycling
- cleaning washrooms and refilling soap, paper towels and toilet paper
- wiping kitchen worktops, sinks and meeting-room tables
- cleaning frequently touched points such as door handles, light switches and lift buttons

Window cleaning at height, carpet extraction and deep cleans are usually outside the daily routine. They are ordered separately and often done by specially trained staff.

Cleaning products have to be handled safely. Employers must assess the risks of the products they use, explain safe use and provide protective equipment such as gloves where needed. Products should stay in labelled containers and should never be mixed.

## Shift patterns, and how a cleaning company in London plans around transport

Because offices are mostly cleaned outside working hours, shifts tend to be short and fixed. Common time slots are, for example:

- early: around 5:30 to 8:30 am, before staff arrive
- daytime: around 9 am to 1 pm, known as day cleaning, while the office is in use
- evening: around 5:30 to 8:30 pm, after the office closes

Some cleaners work split shifts, with one site in the morning and another in the evening. Roles can be full-time or part-time, permanent, fixed-term or temporary. Mobile teams travel between several sites in one shift.

For a cleaning company, London adds its own constraints. Very early starts and late finishes have to fit around night buses and the first trains, and travel between central sites can take longer than the cleaning itself. Some London employers voluntarily pay more than the minimum rate, but they are not required to. Anyone considering split shifts should work out how much time is left between them and whether travel between sites is paid.

## Cleaning agencies or direct employment

Cleaning agencies supply cleaners for holiday cover, short contracts and one-off jobs such as after-builders cleans. Working through an agency is different from being employed by the contractor itself:

- before finding work for you, an agency must give you a key information document that explains the pay rate, who pays you and any deductions
- agencies generally cannot charge work-seekers a fee for finding them work
- you should confirm whether you will be paid by the agency, the client or an umbrella company

Agency work can suit people who want flexibility, but hours may change from week to week. Direct employment with a contractor more often comes with fixed sites and fixed weekly hours. Neither is automatically better; what matters is what is written down.

## Finding an employer close to home

When a business looks for "office cleaning services near me", it usually chooses a contractor that can reach the site quickly. As a result, cleaning firms tend to recruit people who live near the sites they already serve. For cleaners, a short journey matters just as much, especially with a 5:30 am start.

Ways to look for an employer include:

- online job boards
- career pages of facilities management and contract cleaning companies
- local cleaning firms, contacted directly by phone or email
- recruitment agencies that specialise in cleaning and facilities roles

A few quick checks help before accepting an offer. A genuine employer gives its full registered company name, address and site location, and these details can be checked before you start. No legitimate employer should ask for money before you start, whether for uniforms, training or "registration".

## Pay rules in 2026 and what to check in the contract

From 1 April 2026 the minimum hourly rates are £12.71 for workers aged 21 and over, £10.85 for those aged 18 to 20, and £8.00 for 16- and 17-year-olds and for the apprentice rate. There is no separate minimum rate for cleaners and no required premium for night or weekend work; any extra pay for those hours depends on the contract.

A quick check against your payslip: 3 hours a day on 5 days is 15 hours a week, which at £12.71 comes to £190.65 gross. These figures are minimum rates, not offers from any employer.

Most workers are entitled to 5.6 weeks of paid holiday a year, calculated pro rata for part-time and irregular hours. The employer must carry out a right-to-work check before the job starts, and a criminal record check may be requested for sensitive sites.

A written statement of the main terms of employment must be provided from the first day. Before signing, check that it covers:

- the employer's name and the site or sites
- contracted weekly hours and start and finish times
- the hourly rate before deductions and how often you are paid
- whether travel between sites is paid
- holiday entitlement and how it is calculated
- probation and notice periods
- uniform, equipment and protective clothing provided by the employer

Payslips must be itemised and show the hours worked where pay depends on time. If the hours do not match your own records, raise it with the supervisor or payroll straight away.

## Conclusion

Office cleaning in England is organised around short, fixed shifts, usually for a cleaning company rather than the business whose office is cleaned. When comparing offers, it is worth looking beyond the hourly rate: at the journey, the shift times, the contracted hours, the type of sites and the written terms. The 2026 pay rules and the checklist above make it easier to assess any offer calmly.
