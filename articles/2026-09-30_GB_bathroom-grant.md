# Walk In Shower Grants for Pensioners in 2026: What the £30,000 Disabled Facilities Grant Covers

For many older homeowners the bathroom is the room that feels least safe. A high bath side, a slippery floor or a shower over the bath can turn washing into a daily worry. Adapting a bathroom is one of the most common home changes funded by councils, and walk in shower grants for pensioners are more widely available than many people think. This guide explains which adaptations are typical, how the main grant works in 2026, what the means test looks at and how people pay for the rest.

## Walk in shower for elderly users, walk-in baths and smaller changes

Bathroom adaptations range from small fixes to a complete refit:
- grab rails beside the toilet, bath and shower;
- a raised toilet seat or a toilet frame;
- a bath lift or a bath board;
- non-slip flooring;
- a walk in shower for elderly users, with a level or very low entry and a fold-down seat;
- a walk in bath with seat and a watertight door;
- a full wet room with a floor-level drain.

Walk in tubs for seniors suit people who prefer a soak but find it hard to step over a bath side, although filling and emptying take longer. A level-access shower is usually the simpler choice for someone who uses a walking frame or a wheelchair.

Small items are often easier to get than people expect. In England, minor aids and adaptations costing up to £1,000 are normally provided free of charge to people the council assesses as needing them.

## The Disabled Facilities Grant in 2026

The main source of funding for larger work is the disabled facilities grant, run by councils. In England it pays up to £30,000 for adaptations that make a home suitable for a disabled or older person, and councils can add discretionary help above that limit. Wales and Northern Ireland set their own limits, and Scotland uses a different system of support.

Typical work covered includes:
- replacing a bath with a level-access shower;
- installing an accessible toilet and basin;
- widening doors and fitting ramps;
- fitting a stairlift or creating a downstairs bathroom.

Owner-occupiers, private tenants and housing association tenants can apply. Before approval an occupational therapist assesses what is needed, and the council checks that the work is necessary, appropriate, reasonable and practical for the home. In many areas home improvement agencies help with forms, plans and builders.

## The means test and who pays nothing

For adults the grant is means-tested. The council looks at the income and savings of the disabled person and their partner, and the first £6,000 of savings is ignored. The result may be a full grant or a contribution towards the cost.

People who receive certain benefits, including Pension Credit, usually pay nothing towards the work. In most cases the grant does not have to be repaid, although for larger grants some councils place a charge on an owner's home that applies if it is sold within a set number of years.

A realistic timetable matters. From the first call to finished work the process can take many months, so it is worth starting early rather than waiting for an emergency.

## Converting bathtub to walk in shower: typical costs

Converting a bathtub to a walk-in shower is one of the most requested jobs. The final price depends on the plumbing, the floor, the tiling and whether the room becomes a full wet room. Grab rails cost relatively little, a straightforward conversion usually runs to several thousand pounds, and a complete wet room can cost considerably more. Getting two or three written quotes is sensible, and the occupational therapist's report helps builders price exactly what is needed.

Two savings are often missed. Some adaptations made for a disabled person can be supplied without VAT when the supplier is told the work is for that purpose. And if the home gains an extra bathroom used by a disabled person, the council tax disabled band reduction can lower the council tax bill by one band.

## Paying for the rest: savings, charities and quick secured loans

When the grant does not cover the full cost, or the work falls outside it, households usually combine several sources: savings, family help, grants from charities linked to particular conditions or former trades, and sometimes borrowing. Homeowners who search for quick secured loans to finish a bathroom should remember that the loan is secured on the home, so missed payments put the property at risk. Comparing the total amount repayable, not just the monthly figure, and asking about early repayment fees keeps the decision clear.

## Adapt or move? Bridging loans for over 70s and a smaller home

Sometimes the honest answer is that the house no longer works: stairs, a single upstairs bathroom and a large garden add up. Moving to a bungalow or a ground-floor flat with an accessible bathroom can be simpler than a major refit. When the right home comes up before the old one sells, some older buyers use bridging loans for over 70s to complete the purchase and repay from the sale. These loans are short-term, secured on property and expensive if the sale is delayed, so independent advice is worth taking first.

Whichever route is chosen, the first step is the same: an assessment of what is needed. With that in hand, grants, quotes and any borrowing can be compared on facts rather than guesses.
